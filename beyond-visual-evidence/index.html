Beyond Visual Evidence

Revealing and Mitigating Relational Privacy Leakage in Document MLLMs

Beining Xu* Hairui Wang* Jiaxin Wang Changsheng Chen† Anirban Chakraborty

Shenzhen MSU-BIT University · Indian Institute of Science

P Paper </> Code HF Models

When visual evidence is weak, a document MLLM may recover a whole identity relation from memory—not merely a single field.

Our goal Measure the relation. Forget the relation. Preserve the task.
01 · Overview

Privacy leakage lives in the relationships.

Document multimodal large language models are trained to extract structured personal information from identity documents. When the input is abnormal, cropped, blank, noisy, or otherwise weak in visual evidence, the model can fall back on memorized relations from its training data.

This failure is relational: the model may jointly reveal correlated sensitive fields such as a family name, given name, birth date, and document number. Existing single-field audits do not capture the full risk.

02 · Method

Dynamic Relational
Unlearning Framework

DRUF combines a retain branch with a dynamic forget branch. The model is repeatedly probed for currently leaked field pairs; those relations update the forget set, while normal samples protect document KIE capability.

Probe

Detect which sensitive field pairs the current model still reveals.

Forget

Apply Relational Decoupling Unlearning to the dynamic target set.

Retain

Use teacher–student alignment to preserve normal KIE performance.

03 · Findings

Suppress leakage.
Keep utility.

Across three document MLLMs and six unlearning methods, weak-evidence inputs consistently expose relational privacy risks. DRUF provides the strongest leakage suppression while maintaining robust document information extraction.

Key takeaway

Relational privacy leakage is a distinct failure mode: weak or missing visual evidence can activate memorized identity correlations, even when individual fields are not directly visible.
